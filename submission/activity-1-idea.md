The problem: Small shop owners in India give a lot of goods on credit (udhaar) to regular customers, and they keep track of it in a paper notebook. Entries get missed when the shop is busy, the writing is hard to read later, totals go wrong, and there are arguments when a customer says "I already paid you". Khata apps exist, but they need typing names and amounts, mostly in English.

Who has it, where and how often: Kirana shop owners in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh who mostly think and speak in Telugu. Many of them are not comfortable typing, and some cannot read or write well. This happens many times every single day, usually while they are serving a line of customers with their hands full.

The voice-first product: Maata Khata is a khata book you talk to. The owner taps one big mic button and just says what happened, like "Ramuki paalu 20, biyyam 50" (Ramu took milk for 20 and rice for 50). The app picks out the name, the items and the prices, adds the total, and reads it back out loud in Telugu. The owner says "avunu" (yes) to save it or "vaddu" (no) to fix it. Nothing gets saved without a yes. When Ramu pays, the owner says "Ramu 30 rupees ichhadu" and his balance goes down. To check, they ask "Ramu baaki entha?" (how much does Ramu owe?) and the app says the answer out loud. One tap sends Ramu a polite reminder on WhatsApp. It also understands Hindi and English.

Why voice comes first: The owner is already saying these exact words to the customer at the counter. Saying them to the phone takes a few seconds and needs no typing, no English and no reading, so it works for people who would never use a typing app. Hearing the entry read back catches mistakes before they are saved, so both the owner and the customer can trust the book.
